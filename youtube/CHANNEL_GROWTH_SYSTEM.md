# CodeCram — Channel Growth System

**Channel:** CodeCram · @aitunisia-h9f  
**Positioning:** Practical programming, AI experiments, Arduino/IoT builds, and software engineering explained by building real things.  
**Primary language:** English. Use accurate English captions on every video.  
**Audience hypothesis:** Beginner-to-intermediate developers, computer-science students, and curious makers. Validate this against YouTube Studio audience data before treating it as fact.

## 1. Baseline and strategic diagnosis

Verified connected-channel snapshot (21 November 2026):
- 4 subscribers
- 6,152 lifetime views
- 5 public uploads

The two strongest examples in the channel review were short, relatable programming videos (about JSON and the experience of being told coding is easy). This is a useful early signal, not proof of a permanent niche or a guarantee that the same format will repeat.

**Strategic decision:** Use relatable programming Shorts as the discovery engine; use practical, genuinely demonstrated project videos as the trust and subscriber-conversion engine. Do not pivot entirely to advanced AI/security content or publish unrelated topics on the same channel without a deliberate test.

## 2. Channel promise and identity

**One-line promise:** Learn coding, explore AI, and build real projects — one experiment at a time.

**Suggested channel description:**

Welcome to CodeCram — where code becomes something real.

We build practical software projects, explore AI tools, experiment with Arduino and IoT, and explain programming concepts in a clear, beginner-friendly way.

Expect hands-on tutorials, honest experiments, useful coding Shorts, and complete project walkthroughs.

Subscribe and build along.

**Voice:** Curious, energetic, direct, technically honest, and beginner-friendly. Explain jargon in plain English. Show the actual result and the real limitations.

**Content pillars**
1. Programming made relatable — debugging moments, coding misconceptions, language comparisons, student-developer humor.
2. AI in practice — useful AI workflows, coding experiments, AI assistants, responsible limitations.
3. Arduino and IoT — ESP32/Arduino, sensors, connected devices, dashboards.
4. Full-stack builds — React, APIs, Python/FastAPI, databases, deployment and engineering decisions.

Keep each upload inside one of these series so viewers know what they will get next.

## 4. Sustainable publishing cadence

Start with a four-week experiment, not an unrealistic daily schedule:

- Tuesday: programming/relatable Short.
- Thursday: AI or Arduino experiment Short.
- Saturday: project-progress Short or a clip from the current build.
- Every second Sunday: one substantial long-form project video.

If a week becomes overloaded, publish two strong Shorts rather than rushing four weak uploads. Do not upload several videos at once to compensate for missed days.

## 6. Packaging rules

### Titles
- Put the concrete subject and payoff early.
- Make one clear promise.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives, fake speed claims, and “revolutionary” language.
- Use curiosity only when the video resolves it.
- Keep title and thumbnail complementary, not duplicate sentences.

### Thumbnails
- Standard long-form: 1280 × 720, 16:9.
- One focal subject, one readable idea, 2–4 words maximum.
- Use a real screenshot/photo from the project when the video claims to show a real build.
- Do not show a completed physical project if only a simulation exists.
- Review at phone size before upload.

### Descriptions and metadata
- First two lines explain the project and the viewer payoff.
- Include the technologies actually used.
- Add chapters after the edit is final.
- Use a small set of relevant tags; do not keyword-stuff.
- Include source-code links only after the repository and exact branch are public and safe to share.

## 8. Production pipeline

1. Pick one viewer problem or curiosity.
2. Write the promise and the proof the video must show.
3. Verify hardware, software, claims, and any numbers before recording.
4. Prepare a shot list and screen-recording checklist.
5. Record the real workflow; capture failed attempts only when they teach something.
6. Edit for clarity: remove dead time, add readable captions, use purposeful zooms and B-roll.
7. Check audio, captions, mobile framing, and technical accuracy.
8. Create title/thumbnail pair and description.
9. Upload as private/unlisted for review; publish only after final approval.
10. Review performance at 24 hours, 7 days, and 28 days.

## 9. Growth measurement

Track each upload in a simple log:
- Impressions and click-through rate (long-form).
- Engaged views and viewed-vs-swiped-away (Shorts, where available).
- Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- First 30-second retention for long-form.
- Subscribers gained per video and per 1,000 engaged views.
- Returning viewers, comments, shares, and traffic sources.

Do not judge a video from views alone. Compare Shorts with Shorts and long-form with long-form, and compare at similar publication ages. Change one major packaging variable at a time. A small sample is directional, not statistically conclusive.

## 10. First 90-day priorities

**Days 1–14:** Establish channel identity, publish the first two Shorts, record the AI + ESP32 build, and verify the actual hardware workflow.

**Days 15–30:** Publish the first long-form project and three related Shorts. Answer useful comments and turn recurring questions into follow-up videos.

**Days 31–60:** Repeat the best-performing series, improve the opening seconds, and publish a second complete project with a different viewer payoff.

**Days 61–90:** Compare the first two months by format and series. Keep what attracts the right viewers and produces subscribers; retire formats that repeatedly underperform after fair testing.

No subscriber or view target is guaranteed. The operating goal is consistent quality and measurable improvement.

## 12. Publishing and safety boundaries

- Never publish private tokens, Wi-Fi passwords, API keys, personal email addresses, or private endpoints.
- Use only original or properly licensed music, footage, images, and fonts.
- Clearly label simulations and synthetic data.
- Do not claim AI accuracy, real-world detection, or physical hardware behavior that has not been tested.
- This playbook does not publish or schedule content. Each final upload, privacy setting, schedule, and metadata change requires review and approval.
